Normal month-to-month range: units/txn 2.7%, GP/txn 3.3%. Guardrail: if pilot-month units/txn falls more than 3% below the pre-pilot average, the transaction metric no longer reflects profit, so the result is classed inconclusive (revert).

Reading (zero bias, normal approximation): at the zero-profit boundary the naive rule keeps the rise ~50% of the time and the one-sided rule ~5% - by construction, not a validated error rate. What matters commercially: the one-sided rule rarely keeps a clearly harmful rise (12% loss), but it also often rejects profitable ones, especially when the break-even is 7.4% (after a cost fix, or if the 1.43 is an allocation). A 2-month pilot is not more precise than a 1-month pilot here, because the frozen trend drifts; it mainly averages the turn-of-month cycle.
Protocol this supports: ONE calendar month, fixed horizon, one-sided rule with the threshold for the applicable cost case. Anything else - inconclusive range, failed basket guardrail, or cross-check disagreeing - means revert prices. A day-14 safety stop (traffic down > 15%) is an override whose effect on these probabilities is not estimated. The probabilities above describe ONLY the simplified one-month traffic rule. Limits: 4 one-month and 3 overlapping two-month windows; tail risks cannot be validated.
