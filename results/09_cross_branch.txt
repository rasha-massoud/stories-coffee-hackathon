Implication for the pilot: measure Garden's loss AND where lost identified customers go (Customer IDs). The decision rule should be company-level gross profit, not Garden's alone. A Garden-only rule would reject a price rise that makes Stories more money.
Caveat: switching behaviour under a price change is not observed in this data (prices never change). The fact that customers already spread their visits across branches about as much as chance suggests switching is easy, but s must be measured in the pilot.

Top 20% of identified customers (698) bring 33.4% of identified gross profit and 30.1% of identified visits (observed). A strict 80/20 rule would be 80%.
Identified customers bring 63.5% of all gross profit; anonymous visits the rest.
Visits per identified customer in 6 months: median 6, 90th percentile 9, max 18. GP per visit, top 20% vs rest: $5.87 vs $5.04
Reading: value is spread fairly evenly (no 80/20). Top customers are worth more mostly because they come more often (30% of visits) and partly because each visit earns more (ranking by GP also selects higher-GP visits). There is no small VIP group to target; a loyalty scheme should reward frequency.
