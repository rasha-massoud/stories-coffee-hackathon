# 1. Discount weeks
Days where >12% of lines are discounted (normal ~6%): 16 -> 2026-03-12..2026-03-19 and 2026-05-12..2026-05-19
Defined promo windows: 2026-03-12..19 and 2026-05-12..19 (16 days)

## Within-month traffic cycle on non-promo days (controls: weekday, branch, trend)
                    effect vs days 1-7  p-value
C(phase)[T.d08-14]              -0.047    0.012
C(phase)[T.d15-21]              -0.083    0.000

Traffic peaks in the first week of each month and dips mid-month (consistent with a payday cycle - an assumption, the data has no income information). Both promo weeks (12th-19th) sit in the dip.

Discount given in promo windows: $620; all year-to-date discounts: $2,178
First explanation (naive model): promos cut gross profit by ~4% (p=0.03). Challenged: once the mid-month dip is controlled, the traffic effect is -0.2% (95% CI -3.6% to +3.4%) and the profit effect is no longer significant. Conclusion: no evidence the discounts brought extra customers; most of the apparent loss was the normal mid-month dip.
Remaining caveat: events (exams, holidays) in those weeks could still hide a positive effect. Stories should check its own calendar for these dates.

## Monthly customer metrics (observed)

month

6                        20                         2525             4412            2494                        1.75
Total identified customers: 3,494. The ID pool is closed (no ID beyond C03500), and 57% of all IDs appear already in January.
A naive reading says acquisition fell 99% (1,979 -> 20). But a customer can only be 'new' once; with a fixed pool of frequent visitors (avg gap between visits ~24 days), first sightings must front-load. Active customers actually GREW 28% and visits per customer grew 21%; anonymous traffic grew 49%. Using 'first-seen' as an acquisition KPI on this data would trigger a wrong decision.
